## Claude's Features

### 1. The Interface: How You Talk to It

Talking to Claude is like writing a little note to a friend.

There is a big box at the bottom of the screen. You type your request there. You press send. Claude answers in a few seconds.

Your old conversations are kept on the side, like pages in a notebook. You can find them whenever you want.

Claude also has a small **window next to the chat**. When it writes a long text, a table or a little program, it puts it on this "separate sheet." You can read it, copy it, or ask Claude to change it. It is like a whiteboard next to your conversation.

And if you do not feel like typing, you can also **talk to it out loud**. It answers you the same way.

> **A little word to know:** the request you write is called a **prompt**. The clearer it is, the better the answer. "Write a text" is vague. "Write a kind birthday note for my 80-year-old grandma" is clear.

### 2. The Models: Three Brains, Three Runners

A **model** is Claude's brain. There are three main sizes of brain. Picture three runners:

- **Haiku: the sprinter.** It runs very fast over short distances. It is perfect for simple questions and small jobs. It is available in the free version.
- **Sonnet: the all-rounder.** It is good at everything and fast enough. This is the brain you get in the free version, and the one most people use every day.
- **Opus: the marathon runner.** It is slower, but it goes very far. It thinks longer and does better on big, hard problems. It is only available on paid plans.

For everyday use, the all-rounder is often enough. For a very long or very hard job, call the marathon runner.

*There are also even stronger brains, kept for the biggest plans. The names change often, sometimes every few months.*

### 3. Its Big Strength: Reading Very Long Documents

This is its real superpower. Claude is like a **librarian who reads at lightning speed**.

You give it a document. It reads the whole thing. Then you ask questions, and it finds the right pages.

**Example 1: a 100-page contract.**
You have to sign a contract. It is long and full of hard words. You send it to Claude. You ask: "Explain this contract simply. What are the risky parts?" It answers in a few lines and tells you where to look.

**Example 2: a book.**
You have a book to read for school, but you are short on time. You send it to Claude. You ask: "Give me a summary, chapter by chapter." You get the story in a few minutes.

**Careful:** for an important contract or paper, always ask a real person too (a lawyer, for example). Claude helps you understand, but it does not replace an expert.

### 4. Coding and Looking at Images

#### Coding

Coding means giving instructions to a computer, step by step, like a cooking recipe. Claude writes these instructions very well. It also helps find the mistake when a program does not work. It is like looking for the missing piece of a puzzle.

There is even a special tool called **Claude Code**. It is a helper that works directly with the files on your computer. It is only available on paid plans.

#### Looking at Images

You can send it a photo, a drawing, a diagram or a screenshot. It looks at it and tells you what it sees.

For example, you show it a confusing chart. It tells you: "Here is what it means."

**But note: it looks, it does not draw.** Claude can understand an image, but it cannot create one. It is a very good reader, not a painter.

### What Claude Does Best

**1. Writing**
This is its strongest point. Its texts sound natural, as if a real person wrote them. It is very good at long stories, articles and letters. And it keeps the same style from beginning to end.

**2. Reading**
Contract, book, report: it is the librarian who reads everything, fast. You ask questions, and it finds the right passages.

**3. Coding**
It writes programs, finds mistakes and explains what it is doing. With Claude Code, it can even work directly inside your files.

---

### Where It Struggles

**1. It does not make images or videos**
You want a picture of a dragon or a little movie? Claude cannot do it. It understands images, but it does not make them. For that, you need another tool.

**2. It has a few less extras than ChatGPT**
ChatGPT is like a Swiss army knife: lots of tools in one object. Claude is more like a really good pen: it does fewer things, but it does them very well. If you want everything in one place, it will feel less complete.

**3. It can be too careful**
Anthropic wants a safe AI. So Claude is sometimes like a very careful friend: it says "careful!" even for a harmless request, or it says no when it did not need to. If that happens, try explaining why you are asking. It often understands better.

## How Much Does It Cost, and What Should You Pick?

Back to our librarian. The more you pay, the more time they can spend on you.

*Prices are in US dollars, as on Anthropic's website. Taxes may apply.*

### The Three Plans

#### 1. Free: The Library Open to Everyone

**Price: $0.**

- **What you get:** you can chat with Claude, ask it to write, read a document, look at an image or write code. You can also talk to it out loud. It uses the Sonnet and Haiku brains.
- **What holds you back:** there is a usage limit. When you have chatted a lot, Claude tells you to wait a little. The most advanced tools, like Claude Code, are kept for paid plans. So is the strongest brain, Opus.
- **Who is it for?** To try it out, or for small requests now and then.

#### 2. Pro: The Full-Time Reader

**Price: $20 per month**, or about $17 per month if you pay for the whole year at once.

- **What you get on top:** much more usage. The strongest brain (Opus). Claude Code for coding. And other tools to work faster.
- **Who is it for?** People who use it every day. For example, an author writing long texts, a student who reads a lot, a lawyer reviewing contracts, or a developer who codes.

#### 3. Max: The Super-Fast Service

**Price: $100 per month (5 times more usage than Pro) or $200 per month (20 times more).**

- **What you get on top:** almost no breaks, because the limit is much higher. You also go to the front of the line when everyone is using Claude at the same time, and you get new features first.
- **Who is it for?** People who use it all day, every day. For example, developers working with Claude Code. **For most people, it is far too much.**

### Is It Worth Paying?

**Our advice is simple:**

1. **Start with the free plan.** Try it for a few days. It costs nothing.
2. **Switch to Pro if you often hit the limit.** It is the right choice if you write, read or code every day. $20 a month is about the price of two or three coffees a week.
3. **Only take Max if Pro really is not enough anymore.** If Pro stops you every day, then Max makes sense. If not, it does not.

A good rule: **only pay when the free plan truly holds you back.** And if you take Pro, you can pay month by month and stop whenever you like.

## Conclusion: Is Claude the Best Tool for You?

Back to our calm librarian.

Claude is not a tool that does everything. It does not draw, and it does not make movies. But what it does, it does very well: **writing, reading and coding.**

**Is it the best tool for everyone? No.** But if you write a lot, need to understand big documents, or code, it is one of the best choices in 2026. For doing everything in one place, including images, ChatGPT is still more complete.

A common-sense tip: **you do not have to choose right away.** Both have a free version. Try them with the same request, and keep the one whose answers you like best.

And one rule for every robot: **Claude helps, but it does not replace your own head.** It can make mistakes. Always double-check important information, especially for a contract, your health or your money.
